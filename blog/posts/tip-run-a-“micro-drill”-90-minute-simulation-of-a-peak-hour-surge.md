You can't wait for the Diwali rush to find the weak links in your operation. A plan that looks perfect on paper can quickly fall apart under the real-world pressure of a peak-hour surge.

The best way to prepare is to stress-test your facility, your team, and your processes *before* the festive season begins. This quick, low-cost simulation is designed to do exactly that.

## The Tip: Run a “Micro-Drill” \- A 90-Minute Simulation of a Peak-Hour Surge

A Micro-Drill is a controlled, 90-minute exercise that mimics the three most common failure points of any high-footfall environment: a sudden influx of new staff, a critical equipment failure, and an information overload on your supervisors.

Here’s the step-by-step playbook to run one at your facility next week.

### **1. The Staff Surge (First 30 Minutes)**

The goal here is to test your temporary staff onboarding process under pressure.

- **The Action:** Arrange with your staffing partner, like Express HR, to send a small, 10-person "surge team" to your facility. Give your on-site supervisors no more than one hour's notice before they arrive.
- **What to Watch:** How quickly can your team get these new people processed through security, assigned a supervisor, briefed on their task, and deployed to a workstation? ⏱️ Does the shift manager have a clear plan, or is it chaos? This simple test immediately reveals bottlenecks in your onboarding process.

### **2. The Critical Machine Failure (Minutes 30-60)**

This tests your communication and response protocol when essential equipment breaks down.

- **The Action:** At the 30-minute mark, have a designated person place a large **“MACHINE OFFLINE”** sign on a critical piece of equipment (e.g., a key conveyor belt, a sorting scanner, or a primary POS terminal).
- **What to Watch:** How long does it take for the failure to be reported? Who does the operator report it to? Is there a clear, documented procedure, or does panic set in? This drill isn't about fixing the machine; it's about seeing how efficiently your team can report and escalate a critical issue. 🚨

### **3. The Supervisor Overload (Minutes 60-90)**

The final test targets your information and decision-making flow by putting pressure on your floor leaders.

- **The Action:** In the final 30 minutes, have three different team members simultaneously approach the shift manager with urgent but common problems. For example: "We're out of packing boxes at Station 5," "There's a safety hazard in Aisle 3," and "Where does this high-priority order need to go?"
- **What to Watch:** How does your supervisor **triage** these requests? Do they delegate effectively or become a bottleneck themselves? This reveals whether your leaders are equipped to manage the information overload that is guaranteed during the festive peak. 🤯

### The 30-Minute Debrief: Turning Insights into Action

Immediately after the drill, gather all the involved supervisors for a quick debrief. The goal is to create a simple, actionable improvement plan.

Discuss three questions:

1. What was our single biggest bottleneck?
2. Where did our communication break down?
3. What one process change would have the biggest impact?

The meeting must end with a clear action log: **Weak Link | Mitigation Action | Assigned Owner | Deadline.**

This 90-minute exercise will give you more valuable, actionable insights than weeks of planning meetings. It allows you to find and fix your operational weaknesses now, in a controlled environment, before they cost you time and money during the festive rush.

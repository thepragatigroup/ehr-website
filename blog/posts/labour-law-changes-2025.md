India's four new Labour Codes — the Code on Wages, the Industrial Relations Code, the Social Security Code, and the Occupational Safety Code — represent the most significant overhaul of labour law in decades. For warehouse operations, three changes are immediately relevant.

## The Code on Wages: What Changes

The Code on Wages consolidates the Minimum Wages Act, Payment of Wages Act, Payment of Bonus Act, and Equal Remuneration Act into a single legislation. For contract staffing, the most important change is the **universal definition of wages** — which now includes basic pay, dearness allowance, and retention allowance, but explicitly excludes conveyance, HRA, and overtime.

This matters because PF and ESIC contributions are calculated on the wage definition. If your contractor is using the old definition, you may be under-contributing.

## Contractor vs Principal Employer Liability

The Social Security Code clarifies that **principal employers are jointly liable** for a contractor's failure to pay PF and ESIC. This is not new in practice — courts have held this for years — but the codification makes enforcement more straightforward.

What this means: if your workforce partner defaults on statutory payments, your company is exposed. Audit your contractor's compliance certificates quarterly, not annually.

## What to Do Now

- Request PF ECR (Electronic Challan cum Return) copies from your contractor monthly
- Ensure your MSA has a compliance warranty clause with financial penalties
- Ask your HR partner to confirm the wage definition used for statutory calculations
- Keep copies of ESIC registration certificates for all contract workers on-site
